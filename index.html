Full abstract

Dexterous retargeting often requires fitting robot controls to each human hand–object motion, making large collections expensive to process. Offline inverse kinematics provides inexpensive nominal controls, but cannot correct errors caused by contact and object dynamics. We present ReDexT, a two-stage reinforcement-learning framework that learns one residual policy to execute new trajectories from inverse-kinematics commands. The key is to learn reusable feedback by varying both the commands the policy corrects and the motions it practices. We first perturb successful source commands while keeping their motion targets fixed, so the policy learns to correct varied execution errors. Rollout-guided training then broadens trajectory coverage through fresh on-policy learning, without treating rollout actions as imitation labels. Once trained, the shared policy executes new trajectories with frozen weights and supports optional local or shared adaptation for better tracking. On new trajectories screened for initial stability in single-hand simulation, frozen ReDexT achieves higher success and shorter processing times than the evaluated per-trajectory methods. Shared adaptation further raises success from 55.08% to 80.08% across 256 test trajectories. Ablations show that control perturbations ease transfer to kinematic commands, and expanded IK training broadens success coverage. The training recipe extends to four additional robot hands, each with a separately trained policy.

Method overview

We first perturb successful source controls while keeping the reference targets fixed. We then broaden training through fresh on-policy rollouts around the original IK commands. At deployment, the shared policy uses the execution state and upcoming motion to correct IK commands with frozen weights.

Scope and evaluation protocol

We study single-hand manipulation in simulation, with a separate policy for each hand. Bimanual manipulation, sharing one residual policy across hands, and validation on physical hands remain future work.

The experiments use known geometry, simulator state and trajectories screened for initial stability. Sharpa has a floating wrist, no extra actuator force limits and disabled ordinary hand–table collisions. Object-tracking success alone does not establish hand-motion or contact fidelity.

Motion data remain limited in scale and diversity. Frozen SR is 24.51% on unseen objects and 75.32% on seen objects; dataset and motion duration also differ between these groups. Larger datasets and longer, more complex trajectories need further evaluation.

How should the opening flow and videos be read?

The input is desired object, wrist and fingertip motion. ReDexT corrects offline IK commands at each step using the execution state and upcoming reference, while keeping one policy fixed across motions. SPIDER fits controls; CHORD and DexMachina train a separate policy for each trajectory. Each example starts from its own initial state. Videos show six selected executions; the fitting animation is schematic.

Which trajectories enter training and evaluation?

The corpus contains 192 seeds, 473 candidates and 256 test trajectories. Mirrored motions and left/right variants remain in the same split. The test set has 154 seen-object and 102 unseen-object trajectories; all unseen objects are from OAKINK2. Test trajectories stay outside pretraining and configuration selection. Policies are updated on them only during local or shared adaptation.

How are SR, HCR and tracking errors computed?

In the controlled evaluations, a valid rollout must cover at least 99% of the active interval and pass the finite-state checks; terminal padding is excluded. MuJoCo-Warp object and hand metrics compare saved states with reference targets at the same simulation time. SR then requires mean translation error ≤ 0.10 m and mean geodesic rotation error ≤ 0.50 rad. Errors are averaged over available active frames for each active object, then the worst object mean is used. HCR requires a complete active-horizon record with no termination signal before the final step. Instantaneous threshold violations or non-finite states trigger this signal; finite rollouts continue and can still meet SR. Rates weight trajectories equally, retaining failed and unavailable cases. Quality metrics average active frames, then successful trajectories; common-success comparisons use the same subset for every method. ManipTrans retains its native horizon and scoring protocol, as detailed in the paper.

What differs between the default and controlled studies?

The default policy uses a 512–512–256 ELU MLP with translation/rotation/finger coefficients (0.005, 0.10, 1). Bootstrap and continuation diagnostics use (0.004, 0.004, 1.2), unless stated otherwise. These coefficients scale both residuals and training noise. Pretraining comprises 50,000 bootstrap updates and five 10,000-update rounds. Seed continuation restores curriculum state; expansion resets stage-local statistics.

Are method runtimes directly comparable?

Reported processing times include initialization, target-specific fitting and rollout after IK. Offline IK and upstream training are excluded. All evaluation uses RTX 4090 GPUs and all training uses H200 GPUs; workflows and concurrency differ across methods. Frozen-policy and SPIDER times reuse the original 32-trajectory profiles; SPIDER was not reprofiled for physical replay. Shared adaptation averages joint H200 training over 256 targets and adds the reused RTX 4090 execution profile. These are collection-level averages rather than single-request latency. Training costs.

How are the videos aligned?

Each method group uses the same trajectory, camera and IK video timestamps. DexMachina poses are interpolated; the other methods use nearest saved poses. Hand comparisons retain their recorded timing, holding shorter clips at the last frame. All ReDexT and hand-policy videos show frozen execution. IK reference is a kinematic visualization; IK-only in the tables is a physical rollout. Enlarged views show the full frame.

What do the video tracking errors measure?

The curves measure object deviations from the matched IK reference: Euclidean position distance in meters and the exported quaternion residual norm in radians. They follow the displayed poses on the shared video clock and stop at the last recorded sample. These render-frame diagnostics are separate from the paper’s active-frame SR, HCR and ADD evaluations.

Full-pool takes 8 h 7 min 55 s and ReDexT takes 8 h 4 min 11 s for the complete joint run. This target-set learning protocol differs from 256 independent 5,000-update local policies. ADD uses each method's own success set.
